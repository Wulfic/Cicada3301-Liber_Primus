## 1. CRITICAL FINDING: Frequency Analysis Rules Out Simple Ciphers

**Source**: Reddit r/cicada post by MaterialTree7641 (Jan 2022, 60 upvotes)

### Key Observations:
- **Solved pages with Caesar/Atbash** show English-like frequency distribution (steep curve, clear letter frequency ranking)
- **Solved pages with Vigenere** (keys DIVINITY, FIRFUMFERENFE) show a more linear/flattened distribution
- **UNSOLVED pages show EXTREMELY UNIFORM rune distribution** — all 29 runes appear >50 times each, with the ratio between least-used and most-used runes being less than 2:1

### What This Means:
The unsolved section uses a **THIRD type of cipher** that is NOT standard Vigenere or Caesar. The uniform distribution is characteristic of a **stream cipher** or **LFSR-based Vigenere cipher**.

### Proposed Cipher Type: LFSR-based Vigenere
A paper from October 2012 was identified: *"Strong Key Mechanism Generated by LFSR based Vigenere Cipher"* (ResearchGate). This method:
- Produces nearly uniform frequency distribution (matches observation)
- Uses a Linear Feedback Shift Register to generate keystream
- Is thematically consistent with page 56's method (stream cipher using prime sequence)

### Additional Frequency Analysis Findings:
- **"-" (space) and "." (period) are NOT encrypted** — they maintain their plaintext meaning in unsolved pages
- This means word boundaries and sentence structure are preserved
- **Single-rune words must decrypt to "I" or "A"** (the only 1-letter English words)
- This gives us known plaintext-ciphertext pairs at every single-rune word position

### Proposed Decryption Formula:
```
plaintext[i] = (±G(rune[i]) ± F(i)) % 29
```
Where G(rune) is the Gematria Primus value, and F(i) is a **running key sequence** probably related to primes and Euler's totient function. This is consistent with page 56's proven method: `decimal[i] = (decimal[i] - (primes[i] - 1)) % 29`

### F-Skip Mechanism (Vigenere pages):
When applying Vigenere, positions where the rune has Gematria value 0 (the F rune) are SKIPPED — the key does not advance at those positions. This was critical for solving the "Welcome" section.

### 7b. Single-Rune Word Attack
Every single-rune word in the unsolved text must decrypt to either I (value 12) or A (value 4). For each single-rune word at position i:
```
F(i) = (G(rune) - 12) % 29  [if plaintext is I]
F(i) = (G(rune) - 4) % 29   [if plaintext is A]
```
This gives us candidate values for the keystream at known positions, potentially revealing the pattern.
